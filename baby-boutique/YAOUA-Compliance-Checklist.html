Purpose. This document maps each legal requirement for an online store in Qatar to the place on the YAOUA website where it is met, and lists what is still needed from the business owner. Status: Done on website means it is built and visible. Needs owner input means the website has a highlighted placeholder until the owner's official document or decision is provided.

Requirements were compiled from public summaries of the laws below and should be confirmed with the Ministry of Commerce and Industry before submission. This is not legal advice.

2. Documents and decisions needed from the owner

  1. Commercial Registration (CR) certificate: registered name, CR number, owner, activity, address.
  2. E-commerce licence from the MoCI Single Window (MoCI Decision No. 25 of 2026). It must name the website domain.
  3. Home-business licence or municipality approval, if operating from a residence.
  4. Owner's Qatar ID (for the CR and licence applications only; not shown on the website).
  5. Domain name (e.g. yaoua.qa) and business email address.
  6. Customer service contacts: phone, WhatsApp, working hours.
  7. Payment gateway agreement (e.g. a Qatar-licensed gateway supporting cards and NAPS).
  8. Delivery partner, real delivery fee and delivery times.
  9. Product data: final prices, sizes, dimensions, yarn and filling composition (from supplier labels).
  10. Toy safety evidence: test reports or GCC conformity (G-mark) certificate for dolls, rattle and pacifier clip, if available.
  11. Policy confirmation: return period (14 days), faulty-item reporting period (30 days), refund time (14 days).
  12. Logo files, if a professional logo exists.

3. How to fill in the website

Open the website, select Open admin, then Store settings → Legal & business details. Enter the CR number, licence number, registered name, owner, address and contacts once. They then appear automatically in the footer, Seller information page, all policies and every invoice. Highlighted yellow items on the website show anything still missing.

4. Laws referenced

  • Law No. 8 of 2008 on Consumer Protection and its executive regulations
  • Decree-Law No. 16 of 2010 promulgating the Electronic Transactions and Commerce Law
  • Law No. 13 of 2016 on Personal Data Privacy Protection, and NCSA privacy notice guidelines
  • MoCI Ministerial Decision No. 25 of 2026 on licensing e-commerce activities
  • GCC Technical Regulation on Children's Toys (GSO)
